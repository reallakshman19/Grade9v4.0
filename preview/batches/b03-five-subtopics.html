CENTER OF MASS · HEIGHT REFERENCE

01 · A distributed body has one center-of-mass height

Make the reference level and the entire mass distribution visible before changing potential energy.

R3 TB-WPE-ILL-11REPRESENTATION gap remains openSynthetic review

Attempt this synthetic question

A uniform vertical ribbon is 3 m long and has mass 2 kg. Its upper end starts at height 4 m and lower end at 1 m above a fixed floor. It is translated straight upward by 1 m without changing shape. With g = 10 m/s², find the change in gravitational potential energy. Is it correct to use the height of the top end as the center-of-mass height?

Hint 1 / 2

Identify the initial and final **center-of-mass** heights, not only the upper end.

Hint 2 / 2

For a uniform straight ribbon, the center of mass is halfway between the two ends. Use ΔU = M g ΔyCM.

Worked reasoning · synthetic candidate, not an approved source key

Initial center-of-mass height = (4 + 1)/2 = 2.5 m. Final endpoints are 5 m and 2 m, so final center-of-mass height = 3.5 m. ΔyCM = +1 m and ΔU = (2 kg)(10 m/s²)(1 m) = +20 J. The upper end is not the center of mass. This is a synthetic rigid-translation case, not the source chain-slip task.

Show model conditions (source qualification still open)
  • Uniform mass per unit length; vertical orientation does not change.
  • Earth-fixed floor reference y = 0; g = 10 m/s² is treated as constant.
  • The 1 m upward translation is rigid, so every segment rises by the same amount.
Academic and native hold: The original ILL-11 chain problem requires checking mass distribution, table reference, motion, and initial kinetic-energy assumptions. This synthetic model does not settle that source task.
POTENTIAL CURVE · CONSERVATION

02 · Read speed from a potential-energy landscape

A newly authored quadratic model illustrates turning points, a stable minimum and the energy line.

R3 WEP07-03REPRESENTATION gap remains openSynthetic review

Attempt this synthetic question

A 1 kg particle is released from rest at x = 1 m in a one-dimensional frictionless potential U(x) = 2(x − 3)² joules, with x measured in metres. Find the total mechanical energy, the location of maximum speed on the allowed path, and that maximum speed. Does every x-coordinate remain accessible?

Hint 1 / 2

The horizontal energy line is fixed by K(1) + U(1); the initial kinetic energy is zero.

Hint 2 / 2

The maximum kinetic energy occurs where accessible potential energy is least. Use K = E − U and K = ½mv².

Worked reasoning · synthetic candidate, not an approved source key

U(1) = 2(1 − 3)² = 8 J, and the particle starts from rest, so E = 8 J. The potential minimum occurs at x = 3 m, where U = 0 and K = 8 J. Thus vmax = √(2K/m) = 4 m/s. Accessible positions satisfy U(x) ≤ 8 J, namely 1 ≤ x ≤ 5 m; beyond the turning points, K would be negative, which is not physical.

Show model conditions (source qualification still open)
  • One-dimensional motion under a conservative force; no dissipative work.
  • Released from rest at x = 1 m; mass = 1 kg.
  • The quadratic U(x) is a *new synthetic model*, not the NSEP examination potential.
Academic and native hold: The original WEP07-03 uses a different (inverse-power) potential and has source/key custody and a paper-code reconciliation history. The synthetic quadratic curve is not proof of equivalent cognitive demand or academic admission.
MODEL SCOPE · CONSTANT FORCE

03 · Check the constant-force condition before multiplying

The endpoints alone do not tell you whether one force vector describes the whole interval.

R3 TB-WPE-ILL-01CONDITIONS gap remains openSynthetic review

Attempt this synthetic question

A particle travels along the positive x-axis from x = 0 m to x = 2 m. The applied force in the x direction is F(x) = 2x newtons. A classmate calculates work using F(2) × 2 = 8 J. State why this shortcut is invalid and calculate the actual work.

Hint 1 / 2

Compare F(0) with F(2). Would a single force value represent both ends?

Hint 2 / 2

Work must accumulate the local contribution: W = ∫ from 0 to 2 of F(x) dx.

Worked reasoning · synthetic candidate, not an approved source key

F(0) = 0 N, whereas F(2) = 4 N, so taking the terminal force over the entire distance is invalid. Integrating gives W = ∫₀² 2x dx = [x²]₀² = 4 J. The endpoint shortcut produced 8 J because it incorrectly held the force fixed at its largest value.

Show model conditions (source qualification still open)
  • The stated 2x N is the complete x-component of the force along the interval.
  • x is measured in metres, force is along the instantaneous positive displacement.
  • Force changes with position, so the constant-force finite dot-product shortcut is not applicable as written.
Academic and native hold: The R3 ILL-01 source uses a constant force and a three-dimensional displacement. This is a separate boundary test, not a correction of the original stem.
FREE BODY · INERTIAL FRAME

04 · The tension formula needs a complete force account

Name the body, axes, acceleration and every external force before writing Newton’s law.

R3 TB-WPE-ILL-02CONDITIONS gap remains openSynthetic review

Attempt this synthetic question

A 2 kg block rises vertically through 3 m with acceleration 1 m/s² upward. A single taut vertical string pulls it upward, gravity acts downward, and g = 10 m/s². Calculate the tension, work by tension, work by gravity and net work. Which stated condition would fail if another upward external force acted on the block?

Hint 1 / 2

Use upward as positive and list all external forces on the block before solving for T.

Hint 2 / 2

T − mg = ma only if there is no third vertical force; signed work uses the same +3 m displacement.

Worked reasoning · synthetic candidate, not an approved source key

For the two-force model, T − (2)(10) = (2)(1), so T = 22 N. Work by tension is +22 × 3 = +66 J; work by gravity is −20 × 3 = −60 J; net work is +6 J, matching ma × s = 2 × 1 × 3. With an additional upward force Q, the equation becomes T + Q − mg = ma, so T = m(g+a) is no longer justified.

Show model conditions (source qualification still open)
  • The 2 kg block is the system; its acceleration is measured in an inertial frame.
  • Only tension and gravity act; the single vertical string is ideal for this model.
  • The block moves 3 m upward while the acceleration and forces are constant.
Academic and native hold: Do not infer a string tension from ma alone. Source and diagram custody for R3 ILL-02 remain independent review obligations.
BOUNDS · DIRECTION · AREA

05 · The work integral has signed bounds

A force–position graph gives the work only after the path direction and coordinate convention are fixed.

R3 TB-WPE-ILL-03CONDITIONS gap remains openSynthetic review

Attempt this synthetic question

Along a straight horizontal track, the x-directed force on a particle is F(x) = 6 + 2x newtons for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2 m. Find the work if the particle moves from x = 0 m to x = 2 m. What is the work of this same force if the particle instead travels from x = 2 m back to x = 0 m?

Hint 1 / 2

Read the starting and finishing x-values carefully; a graph-area magnitude is not automatically a signed work.

Hint 2 / 2

Forward work is ∫₀²(6 + 2x) dx. Reversing the integration limits changes the sign.

Worked reasoning · synthetic candidate, not an approved source key

The force increases from 6 N at x = 0 to 10 N at x = 2 m. Forward work is the trapezoid area ½(6 + 10) × 2 = +16 J, equivalently ∫₀²(6 + 2x) dx = [6x + x²]₀² = 16 J. Reverse work is ∫₂⁰(6 + 2x) dx = −16 J. No physical force reversal was assumed; only the direction of displacement changed.

Show model conditions (source qualification still open)
  • The force component F(x) is defined in the fixed +x coordinate direction.
  • The straight path follows the x-axis; x in metres and F in newtons.
  • The force is the same position-dependent field during forward and reverse motion.
Academic and native hold: Original R3 ILL-03 is an owner-supplied variable-force illustration. This synthetic numeric model distinguishes integration bounds but does not certify the source figure or a native Conditions component.
