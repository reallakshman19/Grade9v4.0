WPE-N04 · Kinetic energy depends on what is fixed

Unqualified construction candidate · No reviewed QRT task identity · Blueprint draft; WPE topic adapter unselected; momentum warrant missing.

Separate the quantities

For a constant positive particle mass in a specified inertial frame, K = ½mv² = p²/(2m) because p=mv. Holding momentum fixed is not the same comparison as holding speed fixed. Speed is the magnitude of the velocity measured in the chosen frame.

Fresh protected attempt

Two particles have the same momentum magnitude 4 kg·m/s in a single inertial frame. Particle A has mass 1 kg and B has mass 4 kg. Compare speeds and kinetic energies, then explain whether changing the observation frame can change the values.

Hint 1 · ClarifySame momentum does not mean same speed: solve p=mv for v.
Hint 2 · ConnectSubstitute v=p/m into K=½mv², keeping each mass separate.
Worked reconstruction · reveal after attemptvA=4 m/s; vB=1 m/s. KA=4²/(2×1)=8 J; KB=4²/(2×4)=2 J. The lighter particle has four times the kinetic energy at fixed momentum. In a different inertial frame, both measured velocities and kinetic energies generally change; preserve one frame within each comparison.
Diagnose and transferWrong route: equal momentum implies equal kinetic energy. Transfer: if instead equal speeds are specified, K is proportional to mass, reversing the mass dependence in this comparison. The algebra here does not prove momentum conservation or provide a collision model.
