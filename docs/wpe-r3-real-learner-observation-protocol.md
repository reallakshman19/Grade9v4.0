# Real-learner observation protocol · WPE Core1A/Core1B R3

**Status as authored: NOT_RUN.** This is a future observation plan, **not** a study, a claim of student mastery, or educational approval.

## Conditions and safeguards

Use only a child learner with consent from a parent/guardian where required and assent from the child. Explain that the session evaluates the **lesson**, not the child, and that they may stop at any time. Avoid copying identifying personal data, private discussions, or photographs/video into GitHub. Keep only de-identified, minimal results with appropriate permission and a short retention plan. Do not present ChatGPT/browser practice history as secure test evidence.

Run at the learner's pace in manageable blocks, one or two constructions per session, rather than forcing all ten tasks in a sitting. The observer is neutral: no leading correction during the first attempt. Use ordinary language and accept "I am not sure." Record assistance only when requested.

## One source-to-transfer observation per construction

1. **Pre-model explanation:** Before opening Core1A, ask an unfamiliar short conceptual prompt: "What can change, what stays the same, and what would you measure?" Record the learner's words without grading as correct yet. For F04/F08 check shared versus relative motion; F05 local versus endpoint speed; F06 mass arriving each second; F07 endpoint state versus history; F08 energy budget ownership.
2. **Core1A explain-back:** Let the learner use the existing diagram/control, guided anchor and one boundary example. Ask them to redraw the system and identify the conditions which make the equation usable. Note where they reach for a formula without its assumptions.
3. **Task A protected attempt:** Present the original fresh A, then independently record the chosen model, assumptions, signed/represented quantity and a short written derivation BEFORE the worked explanation. Record any optional hint use and first-response changes only if the system explicitly marks them (do not silently overwrite).
4. **Task B unfamiliar setting:** Switch to the independent B (ice skaters, safety cord, water current, circular guide, spacecraft docking). Confirm there is no worked B answer, and have the learner choose a model and assumptions before calculation. Capture the first frozen answer and explanation. Record hint level separately; never copy A's result as the child's B answer.
5. **Self-diagnosis and repair:** After commitment, ask the child to identify their first unsupported physical step—not just a wrong number—and describe why the alternative model fails. Then let them inspect the corresponding authored reconstruction.
6. **Changed-condition transfer:** Before revealing the boundary solution, ask the child to predict how the answer, equation or determinacy changes. Include the preload, wave profile, changed intake area, varying force or unknown thruster as appropriate. Capture the first boundary response.
7. **Post-model explanation:** With the lesson out of view, ask the learner to explain the same physical decision with a NEW qualitative example and articulate one setting where the rule would fail. Compare the explanation with the pre-model statement, not merely with the final numerical answer.

## Interpretation rubric — do not infer mastery from numerical matches

- **0 — Unfounded:** No defensible physical model or conditions; ungrounded formula matching.
- **1 — Prompt dependent:** Can reproduce the anchor after hints but fails to identify its applicability to an unseen setting.
- **2 — Partially transferring:** Chooses the right model but omits a key condition, sign/convention or does not explain why alternatives fail.
- **3 — Independently reasoned:** States system and assumptions, chooses an appropriate model, can derive/represent quantities, catches a model counterexample and revises reasoning when the condition changes, **without pre-answer hints**.

Record **model choice**, **assumptions**, **derivation**, **diagnosis** and **boundary transfer** as separate evidence, not a single opaque score. Compare pre and post descriptions with independent academic judgment. Correct arithmetic alone is not sufficient. If any core concept is scientifically misleading, flag an academic **HOLD** regardless of successful software tests.
