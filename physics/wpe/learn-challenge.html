🧲 Work and its sign

Decide whether a force adds energy, removes energy, or does no work.

01 · LEARN TO REASON Core1A candidate

A guide: the moving crate

A 10 N friction force acts left while a crate slides 3 m right. What work does friction do?

Work depends on the component of force along the displacement. For a constant force, W = Fd cos θ. Because friction and the motion point in opposite directions, θ = 180°.

  1. Choose the motion direction: 3 m right.
  2. Choose the force direction: 10 N left.
  3. Calculate W = 10 × 3 × cos 180° = −30 J.
  4. Interpret the sign: friction removes mechanical energy from the moving crate.
When does this reasoning apply?

Constant 10 N force, straight 3 m displacement, and no change of force direction within this step.

Watch out: Work does not become positive just because the object is moving.

🚀 Momentum and kinetic energy

Explain why equal momentum does not imply equal kinetic energy.

01 · LEARN TO REASON Core1A candidate

A guide: same momentum, different masses

A 2 kg trolley has momentum 6 kg·m/s. Find its kinetic energy.

Start with p = mv and K = ½mv². Replacing v with p/m gives K = p²/(2m).

  1. State the relation: K = p²/(2m).
  2. Substitute p = 6 kg·m/s and m = 2 kg.
  3. K = 36 ÷ 4 = 9 J.
  4. At the same momentum, greater mass means less kinetic energy. If mass stays fixed, the graph of K against signed momentum p is an upward-curving parabola.
When does this reasoning apply?

Classical, nonrelativistic momentum; the stated momentum and mass are measured in one inertial frame.

Watch out: K is not proportional to p; for fixed mass, K grows with p².

⛰️ Energy and where motion is possible

Find accessible regions, identify energy barriers and distinguish K = 0 from impossible motion.

01 · LEARN TO REASON Core1A candidate

A guide: a cart climbing a hill

A 1 kg cart has total mechanical energy 50 J on a smooth track. At a point 3 m above the reference level, find its kinetic energy and speed (g = 10 m/s²).

With no losses, E = K + U and gravitational U = mgh. Accessible positions must have K ≥ 0, not necessarily K > 0.

  1. Calculate U = 1 × 10 × 3 = 30 J.
  2. Find K = E − U = 50 − 30 = 20 J.
  3. Use K = ½mv², giving v = √40 ≈ 6.32 m/s.
  4. At a turning point K can be zero; negative K is physically impossible. For vertical motion with constant g and no drag, velocity changes uniformly with time, not uniformly with height.
When does this reasoning apply?

Smooth track, negligible dissipative forces, fixed gravitational reference, constant g and a particle moving along the connected track.

Watch out: A location with U < E may still be unreachable from the starting side if a higher barrier lies between.

📉 Potential energy and force direction

Read the slope of a potential-energy curve to infer the direction of conservative force.

01 · LEARN TO REASON Core1A candidate

A guide: a sloping potential

A particle has U(x) = 12 J − (3 N)x. What is the constant conservative force along x?

In one dimension, the conservative force component is the NEGATIVE derivative of the potential: Fₓ = −dU/dx.

  1. Differentiate U(x) = 12 − 3x, so dU/dx = −3 N.
  2. Apply the negative gradient: Fₓ = −(−3 N) = +3 N.
  3. The force points toward increasing x, where potential energy decreases.
  4. Specify the interval where this potential law is differentiable.
When does this reasoning apply?

One-dimensional conservative force, differentiable potential over the interval, consistent coordinates and units.

Watch out: The slope dU/dx is NOT Fₓ; the force has the opposite sign.

💥 Explosions and energy transfer

Distinguish momentum conservation from kinetic-energy conservation during an internal release.

01 · LEARN TO REASON Core1A candidate

A guide: two carts pushed apart

Two linked carts, masses 1 kg and 3 kg, start at rest on a frictionless track. A spring pushes the 1 kg cart to the right at 3 m/s. Find the 3 kg cart's velocity.

When the external impulse is negligible, total momentum is conserved. Initial total momentum is zero, so final momenta cancel. Kinetic energy can increase because stored spring energy is released.

  1. Initial total momentum: 0 kg·m/s.
  2. Final balance: (1 kg)(+3 m/s) + (3 kg)v = 0.
  3. Solve v = −1 m/s, so the heavier cart moves left.
  4. Final total kinetic energy is 4.5 + 1.5 = 6 J, supplied by the spring.
When does this reasoning apply?

System initially at rest, negligible external horizontal impulse, spring energy released, nonrelativistic motion.

Watch out: Conserved total momentum does not imply conserved total kinetic energy through an explosion.
