# WPE-C02 · Spring contact, stored energy and motion phases

**Stage:** author-created instructional candidate, not accepted native Core1A/Core1B.  
**Proposed source themes:** EX-M24, TB-WPE-ILL-12, EX-M18. Exact source-task classification is pending.  
**Child's key decision:** an **unattached** spring pushes only during contact; after contact ends, the energy equation changes.

## Core1A — guided learning, not the reserved challenge

**A toy cart released by a spring.** A 1 kg cart starts at rest, touching a horizontal spring that is compressed by 0.20 m. The spring stiffness is 100 N/m. The cart is not attached to the spring and the track is smooth throughout.

1. **Sketch two regions:** Region A is from the compressed position until the spring returns to its natural length; Region B begins after contact ends. At the boundary, a spring that cannot pull the cart no longer acts on it.
2. **Stored spring energy at the start:** \\(U_s=\\tfrac12kx^2=\\tfrac12(100)(0.20)^2=2\\text{ J}\\).
3. **While in contact:** spring energy becomes kinetic energy. At the natural length, spring energy is zero and the cart has \\(K=2\\text{ J}\\).
4. **Find the release speed:** \\(\\tfrac12(1)v^2=2\\), giving \\(v=2\\text{ m/s}\\).
5. **After contact:** the spring has no further horizontal force on the cart. On this smooth track there is no horizontal friction, so it continues at **2 m/s**. Do not keep adding spring work beyond the release point.

**Thinking contrast:** A spring connected to a cart can pull as well as push; this lesson assumes **not attached**. If friction starts on a later segment, include it only on that segment.

## Core1B — unseen challenge (different conditions and two ledgers)

**A spring launch followed by rough ground.** A 2 kg toy cart starts from rest touching, but **not attached to**, a spring of stiffness 200 N/m compressed by 0.10 m. The ground is smooth while the spring pushes and up to its natural length. Immediately after the spring reaches natural length, the cart enters a **0.50 m rough strip**, where a constant 1 N friction force acts against travel. No motor or other horizontal work acts. Does it cross the strip, and what speed does it have at the strip's far end?

### 01 Predict — before calculating
Where does the spring stop acting? Will the speed at the spring release and at the end of the rough strip be the same?

### 02 Attempt — independently construct the model
Draw and label two regions: **contact** and **rough strip**. Write one energy relation for each region; calculate spring energy, release speed, friction work and final speed. Explain how you know when to stop including the spring. Preserve the initial model and reasoning before showing any hint.

### 03 Reconstruct — after commitment
Compare the student's two energy relations with the worked model. Ask: which quantity carries information from the end of the first region into the second?

### 04 Diagnose
Possible errors: (a) applying spring force to the rough strip, (b) applying friction during the initially smooth contact region, (c) treating the cart as maintaining its release speed despite friction.

### 05 Repair
Have the student move a **region boundary** in their diagram if they placed it wrongly, correct only the affected equation and recalculate. Save the repaired attempt distinctly.

### 06 Boundary test
Change the rough strip's length to **1.00 m**, with the same 1 N friction. Would the cart reach its far end with nonzero speed? What happens at the stopping point?

## Author-only reference, not for learner-facing pre-attempt delivery

- Compressed spring: \\(U_s=\\tfrac12(200)(0.10)^2=1.00\\text{ J}\\).
- At the natural length: \\(K_{\\mathrm{release}}=1.00\\text{ J}\\) and \\(v_{\\mathrm{release}}=\\sqrt{2K/m}=1.00\\text{ m/s}\\).
- On the separate rough strip: \\(W_f=-1\\times0.50=-0.50\\text{ J}\\). So \\(K_f=0.50\\text{ J}\\) and \\(v_f=\\sqrt{2K_f/m}=\\sqrt{0.50}\\approx0.707\\text{ m/s}\\). The cart crosses the strip.
- Boundary: after 1.00 m of rough travel the full 1 J is dissipated, so speed has fallen to zero at the end; it does **not** arrive with nonzero speed. A longer strip cannot be fully crossed without extra energy.
- **Reconstruction rubric:** choose two regions, explicitly stop spring work at loss of contact, distinguish smooth and rough sections, calculate energies at the transfer boundary, handle zero-speed boundary correctly and explain what changed.

**Scientific conditions:** no attachment; one-dimensional track, no drag; ideal Hookean spring; smooth launch and constant 1 N kinetic friction only after release. Ignore spring mass and losses during release.

**Separation check:** guide = 1 kg cart, smooth track throughout, identify contact end and constant post-release speed; challenge = 2 kg cart and distinct rough region, with a changed energy ledger. Neither copies a source stem.
