# WPE-C01 · Multi-force work–energy ledger

**Stage:** author-created instructional candidate, not accepted native Core1A/Core1B.  
**Proposed source themes:** EX-M105, EX-M24, TB-WPE-ILL-02 (unqualified links; preserve exact question custody elsewhere).  
**Child's key decision:** the change in kinetic energy is the **sum of the work of all forces**, not the work of one chosen force.

## Core1A — guided learning, not the reserved challenge

**A box crossing a level floor.** A 2 kg box is already moving right at 1 m/s. A child pulls it horizontally to the right with a constant 10 N force, while friction is 4 N to the left. It travels 3 m. Ignore other horizontal forces.

1. **Choose the object and motion.** The box moves 3 m horizontally to the right. Start by sketching arrows for pull, friction, weight and floor support.
2. **Keep each force separate.** Pull is along motion, so its work is \( +10\times3=+30\text{ J}\). Friction opposes motion, so its work is \(-4\times3=-12\text{ J}\). Gravity and normal support are perpendicular to horizontal movement; each does 0 work.
3. **Add before using the kinetic-energy theorem.** \(W_{\mathrm{net}}=30-12=+18\text{ J}\). It is wrong to write \(W_{\mathrm{friction}}=\Delta K\) because friction is not the only force.
4. **Check before and after.** Initial kinetic energy \(K_i=\tfrac12(2)(1)^2=1\text{ J}\). Final \(K_f=1+18=19\text{ J}\); using \(K_f=\tfrac12mv^2\), the final speed is \(\sqrt{19}\approx4.36\text{ m/s}\).
5. **Extend gently to height.** If a box climbs upward by height \(h\), the work by gravity is \(-mgh\). It may be negative even if a motor does positive work. A normal force perpendicular to its path still does no work.

**What to remember:** draw force arrows → determine each signed work → add them → only then connect to change in kinetic energy.

## Core1B — unseen challenge (do NOT display the Core1A worked answer beside it)

**A battery-powered scooter on a short ramp.** A 3 kg model scooter starts from rest at the bottom of a 2 m slope. The top is 0.5 m higher. A motor provides a constant **15 N uphill**, and friction is a constant **3 N downhill**. The ramp's normal force is perpendicular to movement. Take \(g=10\text{ m/s}^2\). Determine whether the scooter reaches the top and calculate its speed there.

### 01 Predict — before calculating
Which will do positive work, which negative work and which zero work? Do you expect the scooter to gain or lose motion energy?

### 02 Attempt — independent first response
Choose the scooter as the system, sketch all four force arrows and complete an **unsigned blank ledger** with columns: force, direction compared with travel, work (J). Calculate net work, then \(K_f\) and speed. Write why each sign was chosen. Preserve this first response before revealing hints or answers.

### 03 Reconstruct — compare models after commitment
Have the student explain which physical quantity is calculated from **total work**. Only after their attempt show the author reference below.

### 04 Diagnose
Look specifically for three conceptual errors: assigning positive work to gravity uphill; treating friction work as net work; and assigning nonzero work to the normal force. Ask the child which row of their ledger caused the mismatch.

### 05 Repair
The child edits the **model or ledger row**, explains the change and recomputes. Store the revised response separately from the original.

### 06 Boundary test — changed condition
If motor force drops from **15 N to 10 N** while everything else stays the same, can a scooter starting at rest climb the same ramp? Explain with forces or energy rather than guessing.

## Author-only reference, not for learner-facing pre-attempt delivery

- Motor: \(+15\times2=+30\text{ J}\); friction: \(-3\times2=-6\text{ J}\); gravity: \(-mgh=-3\times10\times0.5=-15\text{ J}\); normal: 0.
- \(W_{\mathrm{net}}=30-6-15=+9\text{ J}\), so from rest \(K_f=9\text{ J}\) and \(v=\sqrt{2K_f/m}=\sqrt6\approx2.45\text{ m/s}\).
- Boundary: at motor force 10 N, the driving work over the whole hypothetical ramp would be 20 J while friction and gravity require 21 J; total work would be -1 J. Starting at rest, it cannot make that climb without additional energy or push. Along the ramp, motor force 10 N also fails to exceed the 7.5 N downhill gravity component plus 3 N friction.
- **Rubric, not just an option key:** correct system, all four forces, four signed work entries, net sum, kinetic-energy equation, and valid physical boundary explanation. A correct final speed without a justified ledger is incomplete reconstruction.

**Scientific conditions:** constant forces along the two-metre travel, negligible drag, standard mechanical work–energy theorem, ideal motor force. The boundary test is a new physical case, not an admitted examination question.

**Separation check:** guide = horizontal box with no change in height; independent = motorized ramp with gravity and friction, requiring model selection and a full net-work ledger. No original exam question is reproduced.
