# WPE-E06 · Ground push, take-off speed and instantaneous applied power

**Role:** newly authored Core1A teaching + distinct Core1B six-stage practice; **AUTHOR_DRAFT_ONLY / NOT_ADMITTED**, not an admitted original question. **Proposed source theme:** EX-M97 (reader SYNTHESIZE/D3, official original/key correspondence unresolved). The source reader's coaching calculation multiplies **net accelerating force** by take-off velocity and calls this delivered muscle power. For a constant applied upward ground reaction, the actual external mechanical power is **applied force × upward velocity**, including the force that balances weight. These are different physical rates. Flag this discrepancy for independent source/key review; never teach the coaching answer as an authenticated value.

**Learner-owned inference W:** the ground-contact phase sets the take-off kinetic energy and includes the gravitational height gained **during** the push; the free-flight rise determines take-off speed; the **external applied push** and **net force** must not be confused when computing instantaneous applied mechanical power.

## Core1A — guided model: a light toy lifting off

A **2 kg** vertically moving toy starts at rest and is pushed upward at a constant **30 N** for **1.0 m** while in contact with its launcher. Take **g=10 m/s²**, ignore losses, and let motion be vertical.

1. **Draw the push phase:** applied force +30 N upward, weight −20 N downward, net +10 N.
2. **Find the work-energy ledger during contact:** W_push=30×1=+30 J; W_g=−20×1=−20 J; net W=+10 J=ΔK. From rest, ½(2)v²=10, so **v²=10** and **v=√10≈3.162 m/s** at separation.
3. **Keep force distinctions:** net accelerating force =10 N; actual applied force =30 N.
4. **Find instantaneous rates near release:** applied power =30√10≈**94.87 W**; net kinetic-energy rate =10√10≈**31.62 W**. Their difference is gravitational potential-energy gain rate.
5. **Use flight only after release:** after contact, applied push vanishes; subsequent rise =v²/(2g)=10/20=**0.50 m**. Never include an applied ground push in this free-flight phase.
6. **Check:** 30 J of contact input = 10 J take-off kinetic energy + 20 J contact height gain.

**Wrong path:** multiplying net F by v answers net translational kinetic-energy rate, **not** the mechanical power supplied by the applied push. Also, the free-flight rise is not the total rise of the toy.

## Core1B — independent new task: a taller toy launch

A **4 kg** vertical toy starts from rest. A constant upward ground-contact force acts over **0.50 m** while the toy remains in contact. After separation, it rises a **further 0.80 m** under gravity alone. Take g=10 m/s². Infer its take-off speed, contact-phase applied work, upward push force, and **maximum applied mechanical power during contact**. Keep the contact height gain separate from the later flight height.

### 01 Predict
Does the force that supplies energy during contact equal the net accelerating force? Why does the later free-flight height inform the speed at the contact boundary?

### 02 Attempt
Draw phases and arrows. Compute v²=2g h_flight, contact ΔK, contact gravity work, applied work, F_push and P_push=F_push v at the instant of take-off. Record the first attempt and why.

### 03 Reconstruct
Compare the two-phase energy ledger and the distinction P_push versus P_net. Name the first different physical inference, not merely the final number.

### 04 Diagnose
Did you treat 0.8 m as a push distance, omit contact gravity work, multiply the net force by velocity as applied power, or assume applied force persists in flight?

### 05 Repair
Correct the first wrong boundary or force and recompute; save the repair separately.

### 06 Boundary test
Change only the **subsequent free rise to 0.45 m**, retaining 4 kg, contact distance 0.50 m, gravity and all ideal assumptions. Find the new applied force and maximum applied power.

## Author-only worked reconstruction — unlock only after attempt

- Flight height 0.80 m implies take-off v²=2(10)(0.80)=16 and **v=4 m/s**.
- Take-off ΔK=½(4)(4²)=**32 J**; contact gravity work=−mgs=−(4)(10)(0.50)=−**20 J**.
- Applied work must be 32+20=**52 J**; constant applied force **F=52/0.50=104 N**.
- Net accelerating force during contact is 104−40=**64 N**. Applied power increases with speed, so **maximum applied mechanical power =104×4=416 W** at take-off. The net accelerating power there is only 64×4=256 W.
- **Boundary:** if later flight rise is 0.45 m, v=3 m/s, contact ΔK=18 J, gravity still needs 20 J, applied work=38 J, applied force **76 N**, maximum applied power **228 W**. Net accelerating power is (76−40)×3=108 W.
- **Evidence criterion:** explicitly separates contact and flight, includes gravitational work during contact, distinguishes applied and net forces, and explains when power is maximum. A correct numerical answer without the force distinction is incomplete.

**Conditions and custody:** starts at rest; constant applied force over contact; instantaneous power of the applied vertical force, not metabolic muscle power; no drag; applied force vanishes after separation.
