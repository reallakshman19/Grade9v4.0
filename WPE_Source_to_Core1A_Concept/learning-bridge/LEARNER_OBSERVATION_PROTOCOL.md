# Eight-lesson WPE self-tutor · supervised learner observation protocol

**Status: protocol READY; learner observations NOT RUN.** Created 2026-11-03 as preparation for evidence-informed revision. This document is not an academic admission, independent B3/B4/QRT approval, an exam-key verification, or a claim that the eight lessons have been tested with a real child.

**Try the existing student page:** `physics/wpe/d3d4-self-tutor.html` in the repository, or the corresponding GitHub Pages path. **No new test website is needed.**

## Purpose and sampling

The question is **whether a learner can reconstruct the reasoning without an adult providing the solution**, not whether they can guess the multiple-choice answer. Suggested first observation: one child on **C01 (all forces)** and **C02 (spring/contact)**, then one advanced topic only if prior concepts are understood (C05 graph axes, C06 circular motion, C07 flow rate, or C08 fixed force/extension). The complete eight-skill trail is *not* a sensible single sitting for most learners. Expand to other students/skills only after the initial notes are reviewed. Advanced tasks may need curriculum readiness checks.

Obtain the appropriate school/family permission before observing minors. Do not collect names, addresses, contact details, photos, audio/video, or identifiable quotes in this repository. A private observer may keep an anonymized, coded summary according to applicable school policies; do not upload student localStorage, full open answers, or identifying notes to GitHub.

## Procedure (per observed skill)

1. **Set up:** use a normal browser, read the lesson title, and have the child read the worked **Core1A** example. Ask the child to describe the main idea in their own words. **Do not correct yet.**
2. **Predict and Attempt:** ask the child to predict what the separate **Core1B** situation requires. Observe whether they choose system/force directions, phase boundaries, graph operation, rate model, or controlled variable before calculating. Let them type an answer or say “I'm not sure.” Avoid prompting toward the right choice.
3. **Reconstruct:** after first attempt is saved, ask the child to compare their initial reasoning with the newly available worked model. Which **first step** differs? Can they say *why*? The model may be reopened during diagnosis and repair.
4. **Diagnose and Repair:** note which misconception option they choose and whether the written repair changes the causal model, not merely the final number. Help is allowed after documenting whether independent reasoning happened.
5. **Boundary test:** before opening the changed-condition model, ask them to explain **which assumption changed**, predict its consequence and justify the new result.
6. **Short debrief:** ask “Which sentence or diagram confused you?”, “Where did you want a hint?”, “Did you know your work would still be there after a reload?”, and “Was the answer available when you needed to review your mistake?”

## Decision rule for the next revision

Treat **software regression** (browser interactions, responsive layout, draft preservation, answer-reveal order) as distinct from **learning evidence**. Only say “students understood better” if comparable observations actually exist and justify the statement.

Prioritize edits where the learner cannot choose the operation without being told, where answer text becomes unavailable for diagnosis, where typing is accidentally lost, or where an advanced prerequisite is missing. Repeat the affected skill after revision using a **different fresh scenario**; avoid teaching the exact test answer to make the second result look better.

For C03–C08, distinguish a curriculum **prerequisite gap** from confusing UI: a younger learner who has never studied v²/r, power flow, or controlled-variable spring equations should be offered a prior skill rather than marked wrong for an advanced topic.
