# The six-stage Core reasoning method

**Core1A teaches the reasoning; Core1B tests independent reconstruction in a different situation.** This is the central teaching principle of the Physics Blueprint, not merely a UI checklist.

**Status:** author-exposed method description; no QRT or academic approval. [Actual Blueprint](../../templates/physics-blueprint/physics-blueprint.D3-D4.json) v0.2.0 remains DRAFT_OWNER_REVIEW, D3.1 PAUSED, and no WPE adapter is approved.

## The core sequence and its purpose

| Stage | Why it exists | Observable child reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Predict** | Make the initial idea explicit | Commits to a physical model and explains why, before answer reveal |
| **Attempt** | Protect the learner-owned decisive inference | Builds a first answer for a genuinely fresh task, with help exposure recorded |
| **Reconstruct** | Understand the mechanism | Compares original work with full warranted steps, conditions, and checks |
| **Diagnose** | Identify the actual source of difficulty | Locates the first divergence and its cause from the saved response |
| **Repair** | Rebuild, not copy, the solution | Revises the underlying model and explains why the replacement works |
| **Boundary test** | Test transfer to different conditions | Reconstructs a new inference when a constraint or assumption changes |

The Blueprint calls the sixth stage **Edge case**; the WPE practice page calls it **Boundary test**. These names refer to the same reasoning check. All six stages belong in the tile's band-carrying **MAIN** and in each separate instance cycle.

## Core1A is complete teaching, not a hidden answer

The Blueprint's Core1A sections are: **Conventions**, **Before you start**, **Build the idea** (action + reason), **Worked anchor** (different problem, complete answer and check), **Plausible wrong path**, and **Close the idea** (independent exit check). The new Core1B problem must not simply change numbers or names from this anchor.

Define **Y = previously taught law/skill**, **Z = connecting route**, and **W = learner-owned decision for the new task**. The teaching may fully explain Y. The fresh learner must still execute W.

Hints follow the original Blueprint: **H1 CLARIFY** (what matters), **H2 CORRELATE** (connect to Y), **H3 OPEN_THE_WAY** (open Z but do not do W). A pre-attempt hint must never reveal W or the answer. Use correct, scene-matched, accessible S1/S2/S3 illustrations or explicitly no picture.

See the [complete runbook](RUNBOOK.md) and [current eight-lesson self-tutor](../../physics/wpe/d3d4-self-tutor.html). This methodology specifies observable evidence; it does not claim a real child has been assessed.

## Evidence and help exposure

The Blueprint's phases are `GIVENS → ATTEMPT → RECONSTRUCT → ANSWER`. The first answer and its time should be recorded before any hints, worked-answer reveal or linked Core1A support opened from that attempt. Later help and revisions must be recorded separately.

- **Unassisted:** The child supplies the decisive inference W before support, with a confirming exposure history.
- **Helped:** The child submits after hints. This is useful practice, not independent demonstration.
- **Reconstructed:** The child compares with the worked model, so their revised work is not an independent first answer.
- **Repaired:** The child changes a reasoning step, explains why, and retains the original.
- **Boundary:** The child handles the changed condition before seeing its solution.

The Blueprint's evidence states are `DEMONSTRATED`, `UNCERTAIN`, `MISSING`, and `UNKNOWN`. A correct number without an explanation is UNCERTAIN; incomplete exposure records support UNKNOWN. A diagnosis selected from a menu is a self-report, not by itself proof of misconception. A six-stage progress counter is not a mastery rating.

## B1–B7: why six screens are not enough

| Witness | Evidence that must exist |
|---|---|
| **B1 Target** | Exact task and decisive operation, proposed/reviewed QRT, assumptions |
| **B2 Signature** | Core1A and a different Core1B require the same *kind* of inference |
| **B3 Sufficiency** | Actual teaching of prerequisites, action + why, wrong path and completed exit check |
| **B4 Reconstruction** | Fresh task demands a learner-owned decision W rather than a copied computation |
| **B5 Protection** | Preserved initial answer, ordered help exposure, separate correction |
| **B6 Diagnosis and transfer** | Error grounded in the actual first response; causal repair and changed-condition reasoning |
| **B7 Physics and provenance** | Verified laws and conditions, signs, units, matching diagrams, checks and source identity |

A correct numerical answer is not automatically MODEL, REPRESENT, SYNTHESIZE or JUSTIFY evidence. The QRT band belongs to the exact task, not the user-interface stage. Each MAIN task, instance task and real examination question requires its own demand check. A D2 instance can support a D4 MAIN but does not count as a D4 original.

The full Blueprint asks for one Core1A teaching construction per instance, one band-carrying MAIN Core1B cycle combining instances and one Core1B cycle per instance. The existing eight-topic practice prototype has six-stage journeys but is not an independently approved native MAIN-plus-instance tile.

## Worked example: C07 conveyor, with one different fresh task

**Core1A (teaching anchor).** A 2 m/s conveyor receives 1 kg/s of sand initially at zero horizontal speed. Force on the incoming flow = 1×2 = **2 N**. Motor pickup power = 2×2 = **4 W**. New sand gains horizontal kinetic energy at **2 W**; idealized slip heat is **2 W**. The child sees every model choice, its warrant and the 4=2+2 check.

**Core1B (different task).** A 4 m/s belt receives chips already moving right at 1 m/s. The feeder supplies half the numerical belt speed in kg/s. The protected W is choosing a one-second stream model and separating motor power, kinetic-energy change and slip heat.

| Stage | Question for child | What the full model later checks |
|---|---|---|
| Predict | “Do chips already have momentum? Does all motor power become their kinetic energy?” | Explains difference between input and gain |
| Attempt | “Choose the incoming mass each second. Find momentum-rate force and three powers.” | 2 kg/s, gain 3 m/s, **6 N**, motor **24 W**, chip kinetic gain **15 W**, heat **9 W** |
| Reconstruct | “What is the first step that differs from the worked route?” | Full reasoning, conditions and **24=15+9** |
| Diagnose | “Did you count accumulated chips, forget initial speed or assume zero heat?” | Identify a specific error supported by saved reasoning |
| Repair | “Rewrite the incorrect assumption and justify the new model.” | A changed physical inference, not just a corrected number |
| Boundary test | “Belt slows to 2 m/s; chips still enter at 1 m/s; feeder rule remains. Do outputs merely halve?” | New rate **1 kg/s**, F **1 N**, motor **2 W**, kinetic gain **1.5 W**, heat **0.5 W** |

**Why this is not an admitted D3.4 tile:** C07 is authored related practice. EX-M16 has an unqualified source-to-practice join, and its exact speed-dependent original demand still requires authorized QRT and source review.
